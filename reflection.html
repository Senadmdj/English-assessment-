ENGLISH ASSESSMENT - DESIGN RATIONALE

THE THINKING
BEHIND THE MESSAGE.

This reflection explains the choices behind the Paradise View destination campaign.

Paradise View is a destination-level tourism campaign introducing Yangzhou, Jiangsu, China, to domestic and international visitors. It promotes the destination as a whole, celebrates its natural and cultural character, and encourages visitors to support local businesses. I designed the homepage to create interest and guide people towards further discovery.

LANGUAGE AND TONE

I used the headline "Where Nature Meets Serenity" to connect Yangzhou's scenery with a calm travel experience. "Serenity" creates a peaceful mood, while "meets" suggests a connection between visitors and the environment. Calls to action such as "Follow the Water" and "Take the Scenic Way" speak directly to readers. They are memorable and encourage visitors to imagine exploring the destination.

Descriptive language helps the audience picture the setting. References to willow-lined banks, elegant bridges, garden paths and local flavours appeal to sight and taste. I balanced these details with recognisable places, including Slender West Lake and the Grand Canal, so the campaign feels connected to a real location.

REPRESENTATION AND PURPOSE

The website presents Yangzhou as more than a collection of attractions. It includes landscapes, gardens, food culture, heritage and local life. This gives visitors a broader understanding of the destination and recognises the people and businesses that contribute to its character. The campaign promotes the destination, not one hotel or tour operator.

The visitor-story section shows how personal experiences can make a tourism message relatable. Its sample comments are labelled as illustrative, not genuine reviews. This distinction matters because a campaign should build trust rather than create a false impression of visitor feedback.

VISUAL DESIGN AND MULTIMODAL CHOICES

I chose blue, white and green to create a fresh identity. Blue suggests water and openness, white keeps the page readable, and green connects the design with nature. Dark teal sections provide contrast, while lighter backgrounds give the content room to breathe. The palette is repeated across buttons, cards, headings and the footer for consistency.

Large photographs establish a sense of place, while six embedded video panels add movement and variety. Short captions explain what each film contributes, so the videos support the written message. Clear headings, simple navigation and responsive layouts help visitors use the site on desktop and mobile screens.

AUDIENCE AND EFFECTIVENESS

The intended audience includes domestic Chinese visitors and international travellers interested in scenery, culture, food and relaxed experiences. I used straightforward language to make the message accessible. The page follows a clear order: it introduces the destination, highlights experiences, presents films and visitor stories, then invites people to explore further.

LIMITATIONS AND NEXT STEPS

Paradise View is a campaign concept, not a live booking service. Opening hours, prices, access details and business information must be checked with reliable local sources before publication. Canva controls whether videos are publicly viewable, and browsers may restrict autoplay. Sample testimonials must be replaced with genuine comments shared with permission. Overall, the project shows how language, images, video and layout can present Yangzhou in a clear, positive and respectful way.
